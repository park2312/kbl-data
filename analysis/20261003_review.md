## Jung Kwan Jang 88-85 Mobis
Official:
- JKJ 26 AST, 6 TO; Mobis 16 AST, 16 TO.
- Mobis won rebounding 40-28.
- Mooney 25/10/4/3 STL/0 TO; Thomas 20/6/0 TO.
- Byun Jun-hyung 14/10 AST; Moon Yu-hyun 8/8 AST/0 TO.
- Prim 33; Bazley 20/12.
- Mooney+Thomas vs Prim+Bazley shared stint: 16:47, JKJ 47-28 (+19).

Interview/report layer:
- Pregame Yoo Do-hoon emphasized transition control and rebounding. He also disclosed Rashawn Thomas had knee pain, making Thomas's near-term condition a recurring preview variable.
- Yoo specifically prepared for Mobis transition by emphasizing ball-line defense, backcourt coverage and help to prevent numerical disadvantages.
- Yoo also identified Park Jung-woong as an important secondary creator and defensive wing if the primary guards struggled.
- Pregame Mobis context: Lee Woo-seok joined only shortly before the opener after early military discharge. Yang Dong-geun still started him, but both coach and player acknowledged that timing/chemistry with the team and the two-foreign-player units would need adjustment.
- Postgame Yoo said Mobis was clearly stronger than last season because Lee Woo-seok had returned and the foreign-player combination was good. Despite the win, he was dissatisfied with JKJ's offensive rebounding and said both the prepared defense and overall offense still needed work.
- Postgame Yang Dong-geun said the game made him rethink several things, specifically the foreign-player types/combination and the time Lee Woo-seok needs to adapt. He also said the prepared defense produced many errors and turnovers appeared whenever Mobis needed to survive a crisis.
- Game-report layer: JKJ recovered from an early 2-14 deficit, changed the game in Q2 behind Byun Jun-hyung, then won the Q2-Q3 foreign-player phase despite Prim and Bazley's raw scoring. User live/postgame observation adds that Byun Jun-hyung attacked Park Mu-bin successfully in Q2 and Mobis later changed the primary matchup to Cho Han-jin; treat the direct assignment as an observation to verify with video/reporting rather than lineup data alone. In Q3, Mooney and Thomas combined efficient interior/passing play while Thomas attacked Bazley one-on-one; the report described this as a psychological/tactical pressure point on Bazley.
- Mobis rallied late with Prim and Park Mu-bin, but the comeback did not erase the structural issues above.

Future checks:
- For Mobis, do not equate Prim+Bazley combined points with lineup quality. Their shared stint net and decision quality matter more.
- Treat the foreign-player combination as an active coaching question, not a settled strength; check whether Yang changes role distribution, spacing, or which player initiates offense.
- Track Mobis turnovers specifically during opponent runs and after defensive breakdowns.
- Track Lee Woo-seok's integration over the next several games rather than judging his ceiling from this opener.
- For JKJ, raise the value of guard/foreign-player connectivity; Byun Jun-hyung and Moon Yu-hyun created a low-turnover structure around Mooney/Thomas.
- Do not overrate JKJ from the win alone: Yoo himself said the prepared defense and offense were not yet fully executed, and offensive rebounding remained weak.
- Recheck Thomas's knee status before every near-term preview.
- Keep the JKJ-Mobis matchup history (now eight straight JKJ wins) as context, but do not use the streak alone as predictive evidence.
